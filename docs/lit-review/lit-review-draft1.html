Literature Review

Guiding Mood Through Sound — two HCI studies of sound-based emotion regulation

Advanced Human Computer Interaction – Literature Review, Draft 1
Project context

Guiding mood, not just setting it

1

Every ambient sound gets a place on an affect map

We extend Moodist, an open-source ambient-sound mixer, by placing each of its 84 sounds on Russell's (1980) two axes: valence (unpleasant → pleasant) and arousal (sleepy → energised).

2

A transition engine walks the listener from current to target

The user marks where they are and where they want to be; the engine moves through the map, continuously cross-fading nearby sounds along the path. No tracks, no lyrics, no song boundaries.

3

Pilot: guided path vs. direct arrival

Same target, same duration; only the route differs. Primary outcome is the experience of the transition (coherence, sense of direction, preference); self-reported affect is secondary.

Figure. The two study conditions on the valence–arousal map. Grey dots are individual ambient sounds; the mix at any moment blends the sounds nearest the moving position.

01
Article 1 · Mood-dynamic playlists   EvoMUSART 2024 · LNCS 14633

A playlist as a path through valence–arousal space

Gaur & Donnelly (2024) — a two-stage algorithm steps a playlist evenly from a start song to a destination song.

1

Stage 1 — aim one step along the line

The target is one even step from the current song toward the destination (their Eq. 1). A K-nearest-neighbour search returns the K songs closest to it in valence–arousal space.

2

Stage 2 — keep the sound coherent

Among those K, pick the song whose audio features (Spotify / Million Song Dataset) best match the step — so mood moves without genre whiplash.

3

Evaluated as geometry, not with listeners

Deezer 2018 (18,644 songs, only 2,762 unique points) → 120,000 playlists per setting. Smoothness = how linear the path is; evenness = variance of step sizes.

Figure 2 from Gaur & Donnelly (2024). A generated playlist stepping from sad (−valence, −arousal) to happy, excited songs — each dot is one song.

Article 1 · Mood-dynamic playlists   Evaluation & results

Path quality depends on the map and on K, not on the metric

DataDeezer 2018 (18,644 songs) + Spotify (15) & MSD (78) audio features
Test12-song playlists; 120,000 per parameter across 12 quadrant pairs
MetricsSmoothness = Pearson r of the path; evenness = step-size variance
1

Euclidean / Manhattan win in audio space

Smoothest and most even audio paths. The stage-2 metric has no effect on mood — stage 1 has already fixed it.

2

The map matters more than the metric

The smoothest playlists run between the two dense regions (sad → happy); sparse regions risk jarring jumps.

3

K is the dial — no listeners yet

Raising K trades mood smoothness for audio smoothness. Proposed next: listeners report mood, listen, report after each song.

Figures 6 and 1 from Gaur & Donnelly (2024). Left — the K dial: as K rises, mood smoothness (solid) falls while audio smoothness (dashed) rises. Right — where the songs are: dense "happy" and "sad" clusters, sparse elsewhere.

Article 2 · Mediscape   OzCHI 2020

An ocean soundscape that responds to your brainwaves

Cochrane, Loke, Campbell & Ahmadpour (2020) — Mediscape is an interactive ocean soundscape, modulated live by EEG data, that teaches breath awareness to novices in a 25-minute session.

1

Problem: novices struggle to start

Mindfulness meditation helps manage stress, but beginners find it hard to establish a practice. Sound is used as a guide, not a reward.

2

A continuous soundscape, not a playlist

Waves, birds and wind. A Muse EEG headband infers the meditation state (busy → relaxed → optimal). As the person settles, the waves slow toward a 4-second breathing rhythm and birds and wind fade; get distracted and they return.

3

Two jobs for the sound

Rhythmic entrainment of breathing (the wave is the breath), and feedback on the inferred state — wrapped in an immersive environment.

Figure. The Mediscape loop: the soundscape shapes breathing, and the listener's state shapes the soundscape.

Figure 1 from Cochrane et al. (2020). A novice practitioner with the Muse headset and headphones.

Article 2 · Mediscape   Study & results

20 novices, one session: less stress, more wellbeing

DesignResearch through design; one 25-min session
Participants20 novices (11 women, 9 men), 19–40
DataPSS + WHO-5 before / after; interviews
1

Measurable change after one session

A 5-minute guided intro, then 15 minutes with the soundscape. Stress and wellbeing both improved, before → after (paired t-tests, p < .01).

2

Four themes in how it felt

Shifting state, attention, self-regulation strategy, immersion. 16 of 19 felt relaxed — but most rode the rhythm rather than reading it as feedback.

3

Three preliminary design guidelines

(1) Structure for focus vs mind-wandering; (2) emulate the breath's rhythm in sound; (3) predictability — balance information against immersion.

06
Insights & implications

What this means for Guiding Mood Through Sound

1

"Aim one step at a time" is a spec for our engine

Gaur & Donnelly's rule — target = current + (destination − current) / steps left, then the nearest songs — is what our tick driver + nearest-k kernel mix does, continuously and for ambient sounds.

← Gaur & Donnelly
2

Audit the affect map's density before the pilot

Sparse regions produce uneven jumps. Our 84 sounds are unevenly spread, so we should check coverage along each study path and avoid routes that cross empty space.

← Gaur & Donnelly
3

Measure the path, not only the feeling

Smoothness and evenness can be computed from our event log for free; pairing them with perceived coherence tells us whether a "wrong-sounding moment" was a real jump.

← Gaur & Donnelly
4

Continuous ambient sound can shift state on its own

Mediscape moved stress and wellbeing with one modulated scene — no music, no lyrics. Ambient mixing is a credible medium; measure immersion and coherence, which its participants talked about most.

← Mediscape
5

Our pilot is the listener test both papers leave open

Gaur & Donnelly's proposed next study — report mood, listen, report after each step — is our probe design. Mediscape has people but no path comparison. Lead with preference, perceived direction and coherence; report affect descriptively.

← both
6

Information must not break immersion

Mediscape's third guideline: balance information vs immersion — its listeners ignored the state feedback and rode the rhythm. Ours should be audibly going somewhere, yet still sound like one scene.

← Mediscape
