Article 1   Gaur & Donnelly, 2024 · EvoMUSART, LNCS 14633

A playlist as a path through mood space

An algorithm that walks a playlist from a start song to a destination song, one even step at a time.

1

Aim one step toward the destination

Each song moves the same distance along a straight line in valence–arousal space.

2

Pick the song that sounds closest

Of the K nearest songs to that point, take the one whose audio best matches the last — so the mood shifts without genre whiplash.

A 12-song path from sad to happy. Each dot is one song. — Fig. 2, Gaur & Donnelly (2024)

Article 2   Cochrane, Loke, Campbell & Ahmadpour, 2020 · OzCHI

Mediscape: an ocean that follows your brainwaves

A continuous soundscape — waves, birds, wind — that helps novice meditators settle into a breathing rhythm. No music.

1

It listens to you

A Muse EEG headband reads how settled you are: busy → relaxed → optimal.

2

You settle, it settles

The waves slow to a 4-second breathing rhythm; birds and wind fade away. Drift, and they return.

The loop: the soundscape shapes your breathing, and your state shapes the soundscape.

04
Article 2   What they found

One 25-minute session: less stress, more wellbeing

20 novices. Questionnaires before and after, then an interview.

Perceived stress · PSS, 40 = no stress
37 → 39
Less stress · t(19) = 3.82, p < .01
Wellbeing · WHO-5, 0–100
60 → 80
Higher wellbeing · t(19) = 4.03, p < .01
!

People followed the rhythm, not the feedback

16 of 19 felt relaxed — but most simply breathed with the waves rather than reading them as a signal about their state. Hence the authors' guideline: balance information against immersion.

05
How the articles relate

One has the path. The other has the listeners.

Gaur & Donnelly (2024)

Has the path

  • Discrete songs
  • Steered by a chosen destination
  • Evaluated as geometry
  • Never played to a listener
Cochrane et al. (2020)

Has the listeners

  • One continuous ambient scene
  • Steered by EEG
  • Evaluated with 20 people
  • No path to compare against
Both treat mood as a position that sound can move through gradually. Neither plays a guided path to listeners, and neither moves a mix of ambient sounds across an affect map. That is our pilot.
06
Implications

What this means for our pilot

1

Build the path one step at a time — on a dense map

Gaur & Donnelly's rule is what our engine does. Sparse regions will jump, so check coverage along each study path before we test.

2

Keep it one scene

Mediscape's listeners ignored the feedback and followed the rhythm. Our transition should be audibly going somewhere without breaking immersion.

3

Run the listener test both papers leave open

Report mood, listen, report again. Lead with preference, sense of direction and coherence; report affect change descriptively.
